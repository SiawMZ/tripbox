- Sensor Tower (Oct 2025): in SEA, non-game apps overtook games as the top revenue genre; users spent ~1.2× more on non-games than on games in 2025 [S15].
- SEA game IAP revenue was $625M in Q1 2025 (7th globally); Thailand led at $162M. Malaysia was not quantified [S16].
- **Gap:** I found no public Malaysia-specific 2025 consumer app spend figure. Statista has a paywalled series [S17].

## 3. Monetization options and unit economics

### 3.1 Options

| Model | Evidence | Fit for Linkbox |
|---|---|---|
| Freemium, unlimited free core + paid power features | Raindrop: unlimited free bookmarks; Pro gates full-text search, permanent copies, backups [S22] | Strong precedent. AI labels / AI search are natural paid features because each one has a per-use cost. |
| Freemium with caps (e.g. N AI labels/month) | Common pattern; no SEA-specific data found | Matches the cost structure: the LLM cost scales with saves. |
| Subscription | IN/SEA medians: $3.75/mo, $18.32/yr; D35 conversion 0.7–1.4% [S18] | Low conversion, low price. Viable only at volume. |
| One-time purchase / lifetime | mymind lists $299 lifetime (aggregator) [S24] | Simple, but conflicts with recurring LLM and hosting costs. |
| Ads | Vercel Hobby forbids ads and payments (commercial use) [S26] | Small revenue for a utility app, and it forces a Vercel plan change. |
| Donations | Vercel explicitly says donations are **not** commercial use [S26] | The only monetization compatible with staying on Hobby. |

### 3.2 AI label cost per save (Claude Haiku 5.5, the model the repo uses: `claude-haiku-5-5` in `supabase/functions/enrich/index.ts`)

Prices (prompts ≤100K tokens): input $0.10/MTok, output $0.50/MTok, cache hit $0.01/MTok. Batch API: $0.05 / $0.25 [S27][S28]. Legacy Haiku 4.5 is $1 / $5 (10× more).

**ESTIMATE** (token counts are assumptions, not measured; check real usage in the Anthropic console):
- Base: 1,500 input + 150 output tokens per save → 1,500×$0.10/1M + 150×$0.50/1M = $0.00015 + $0.000075 = **$0.000225/save** (~RM0.001).
- High: 4,000 in + 400 out → $0.0004 + $0.0002 = **$0.0006/save**.
- Ask AI query (assume 20,000 in + 500 out) → $0.002 + $0.00025 = **$0.00225/query**.

### 3.3 Free-tier limits (as of Oct 2026)
- **Supabase Free** [S29]: 500 MB database per project; 50,000 MAU; 1 GB file storage; 5 GB egress (+5 GB cached); 500,000 Edge Function invocations; pauses after 1 week of inactivity; 2 active projects.
- **Supabase Pro**: from $25/mo; 8 GB disk, 100k MAU, 100 GB storage, 250 GB egress, 2M invocations, $10 compute credit [S29].
- **Vercel Hobby** [S26]: 100 GB Fast Data Transfer, 1M function invocations, 10 GB origin transfer, 4 h active CPU per month; **non-commercial personal use only** (payments, ads, sale of services are all "commercial").

### 3.4 What breaks at 1k / 10k / 100k registered users. **ESTIMATE, all assumptions labelled**

Assumptions (illustrative, not measured):
- 40% of registered users active monthly.
- 30 saves per active user per month, plus 5 Ask AI queries.
- ~200 stored links per registered user after a year.
- ~2 KB of DB space per link including indexes.
- ~30 KB per stored thumbnail.
- Each active user views ~300 thumbnails a month.

| Resource (limit) | 1k users | 10k users | 100k users |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active users (×0.4) | 400 | 4,000 | 40,000 |
| Saves/mo (×30) | 12,000 | 120,000 | 1,200,000 |
| AI label cost (base–high) | $2.70–7.20 | $27–72 | $270–720 |
| Ask AI cost (×5 × $0.00225) | $4.50 | $45 | $450 |
| **LLM total / month** | **~$7–12** | **~$72–117** | **~$720–1,170** |
| DB size (users × 200 × 2 KB) vs 500 MB | 0.4 GB, **near limit** | 4 GB, **exceeds** | 40 GB, **exceeds Pro's 8 GB** |
| Thumbnails (users × 200 × 30 KB) vs 1 GB | 6 GB, **exceeds** | 60 GB | 600 GB, exceeds Pro's 100 GB |
| Egress (active × 300 × 30 KB) vs 5 GB | 3.6 GB, near | 36 GB, **exceeds** | 360 GB, exceeds Pro's 250 GB |
| Edge invocations (saves + asks) vs 500k | 14k ok | 140k ok | 1.4M, **exceeds** (Pro covers 2M) |
| Auth MAU vs 50k | ok | ok | 40k, near limit |
| Vercel Hobby | ok technically; any monetization breaches ToS | same | the 100 GB transfer is likely still fine for a static web app; ToS same |

Reading the table:
- Thumbnail storage, then DB size, are the first free-tier limits to break, around **~1k users**.
- At ~10k users, Supabase Pro ($25+/mo) plus ~$100/mo LLM spend is the realistic floor.
- The LLM cost itself is small per user: about $0.03/MAU/month in the high case (1,170/40,000). This assumes Haiku 5.5 pricing holds.

**Break-even illustration (ESTIMATE):** 10k downloads × 0.7–1.4% D35 conversion = 70–140 payers. At the IN/SEA median of $18.32/yr minus the 15% Play fee, that is ≈ $1,090–2,180/yr. The hosting + LLM cost at 10k users is ≈ ($25 + $72–117)×12 ≈ $1,160–1,700/yr. Roughly break-even, so a paid tier only pays if conversion beats the regional median or costs are capped (e.g. AI on paid tier only).

## 4. Publishing and regulatory

### 4.1 Google Play
- **Closed testing (verified on Google's help page):** personal developer accounts created after **13 Nov 2023** must run a closed test with **≥12 testers opted in continuously for ≥14 days** before applying for production access [S30].
  - Third-party guides add that the clock resets if testers drop below 12, and that Google checks for real engagement. These are **UNVERIFIED** and come from vendors selling tester services [S31].
  - Organization accounts are exempt (third-party claim).
- **Android developer verification (new):**
  - Per Google: developer APIs and "limited distribution" accounts launched Aug 2026; protections begin **30 Sep 2026 in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore, Thailand**, expanding globally in **2027+** [S32].
  - Limited-distribution (hobbyist) accounts can share with up to 20 devices without ID or fee [S32].
  - **Implication:** the current sideloaded APK route will need a registered developer identity once Malaysia is covered (2027+). Indonesian/Singaporean/Thai users already face this now.
- Registration fee: not verified in this research (Google's page points to its FAQ).

### 4.2 Malaysia PDPA 2010 and the 2024 amendment
- **Phases:** in force in three phases (1 Jan, 1 Apr, 1 Jun 2025) [S33].
  - Apr 2025: "data user" renamed "data controller"; biometric data becomes sensitive; data processors directly bound by the security principle; whitelist for cross-border transfer removed (transfers allowed to jurisdictions with substantially similar law or equivalent protection); max fine raised to **RM1,000,000** (from RM300,000) and 3 years' jail [S33].
  - Jun 2025: mandatory DPO, breach notification, data portability [S33].
- **Breach notification guideline (effective 1 Jun 2025):** notify the Commissioner as soon as practicable and **within 72 hours** where a breach causes or is likely to cause significant harm (or affects >1,000 data subjects). Notify affected individuals within **7 days** after notifying the Commissioner. Keep a breach register for 2 years [S34][S35].
- **DPO thresholds:** a DPO is required when processing >20,000 data subjects, >10,000 for sensitive data, or regular/systematic monitoring. Notify the Commissioner within 21 days. **Single vendor source; verify against the PDP guideline** [S34].
- **Relevance to Linkbox (my reading, not legal advice):**
  - The PDPA applies to personal data processed "in respect of commercial transactions". A free, non-monetized personal app may arguably fall outside it. Once monetized or published at scale, assume it applies. **Uncertain; get advice.**
  - The DPO duty would plausibly trigger at ~20k users.
  - Hosting is outside Malaysia (Supabase/Vercel regions), so the cross-border transfer rule applies.
  - Linkbox stores emails, saved URLs and notes. Saved links can reveal sensitive interests (health, religion, politics), which raises the stakes of a breach.
  - Existing account deletion (the `delete-account` function) supports data-subject rights.

### 4.3 Platform ToS risks for fetching previews
- **Instagram/Facebook:** Instagram's help centre states its Terms prohibit collecting data by automated means without permission, and it blocks scraping patterns [S36]. Meta's official route is **Meta oEmbed Read**, which requires **App Review and business verification** [S37]. Since 3 Nov 2025, oEmbed no longer returns `thumbnail_url` or `author_name` [S37]. A personal (non-business) developer may not qualify, and even approved oEmbed gives no thumbnail. Server-side fetching of IG pages is therefore both technically fragile (login walls, which the app already handles) and against the ToS.
- **TikTok:** a public oEmbed endpoint (`tiktok.com/oembed?url=`) returns title, author and `thumbnail_url`. The docs show no credential in the example request. Embeds must use TikTok's player and link back [S38]. This is the lowest-risk platform. Storing a copy of the thumbnail (vs hot-linking) is not addressed. **Check the TikTok Developer Terms.**
- **Xiaohongshu:** no official user agreement text retrieved. Scraper vendors state XHS terms restrict automated access [S39]. **UNVERIFIED.** Treat as high risk.
- **YouTube:** not researched in this pass; it has a public oEmbed.
- **General:** ToS breaches are mainly contract risk (account/IP blocks, rarely civil suits), per commentary from vendors with an interest in scraping [S36-comment]. Not legal advice. Storing copies of creators' thumbnails also raises copyright questions if the app is published.
